SafeRoute AI

A road-risk API for Egyptian and Saudi highways. It scores how dangerous a stretch of road is, then turns that score into something people can act on: a costed site report for the road authority, a safer route for the trip, and a spoken curve warning for the driver.

Role
Sole engineer: data, models, API, UI
Stack
PyTorch, Transformers, YOLOv8, FastAPI, OSM, Sentinel-2, SRTM
Code
github.com/YoussefKabbary/SafeRoute-AI ↗

What I built

  • Four independent risk signals: AASHTO geometry physics, a Transformer crash predictor, Sentinel-2 hazards (flooding, sand, roadside canals) compared against the same road a year earlier, and YOLOv8 pavement defects.
  • Site reports for road authorities: an A4 report in Arabic with English alongside. Treatments come from published crash modification factors (FHWA, AASHTO HSM), costed in EGP ranges and staged from "this week" to "budgeted works".
  • Safer routing: a driving graph built from OpenStreetMap and scored in risk-kilometres over 300 m segments, which trades travel time against exposure to dangerous road.
  • Driver warnings designed against alarm fatigue: the app stays silent unless the warning changes what the driver should do. All of Cairo comes down to 13 warnings from 1,055 measured segments, in a 5 kB pack that works offline.
  • No fake confidence: a model that can't run contributes nothing rather than a placeholder 0.5, and every response lists the models that ran.

Crowd Master

Real-time crowd analytics for CCTV, webcams, RTSP cameras and phones: people counting, doorway entry/exit, zone occupancy, heatmaps and anomaly clips. v2 is my rebuild of v1 after I audited it line by line.

Role
Sole engineer: pipeline, tracking, API, audit
Stack
YOLOv8 (detect + pose), ByteTrack, PyTorch, OpenCV, ONNX, FastAPI
Code
github.com/YoussefKabbary/Crowd-Intelligence-System ↗

What I built

  • Counting that survives occlusion: YOLOv8 body detections fused with pose-derived head points, tracked with ByteTrack in a multi-threaded pipeline.
  • Operational outputs: per-zone peak and average occupancy, anomaly detection (density surges, running, rapid evacuation) with saved clips, 5 s–5 min occupancy forecasts, and a PDF/CSV session report.
  • The audit: 28 documented defects, several of them silent. Sliced inference threw away every track ID, which quietly disabled door counting. The panic detector was fed a constant image, so it could never fire.
  • v3 watchlist layer: face, re-ID and gait matching that first checks whether the camera's pixels can support each method. It won't ship until its false-accept rate is measured.

Known limits & next step

  • All published figures are speed figures. Accuracy against hand-counted footage is next, and the harness for it is already built.
  • Gate counting is reliable for single-file crossings, not for dense two-way flow.

Smart Aquarium

Automated aquarium maintenance on an Arduino Nano: turbidity sensing, a motorised cleaning net and a servo feeder. Two Python desktop apps drive it over USB serial and HC-05 Bluetooth at the same time, using one protocol.

Role
Firmware, wiring, protocol, both apps
Stack
Arduino C++, L298N, HC-05, Python, Tkinter
Code
github.com/YoussefKabbary/Smart-Aquarium ↗

What I built

  • Firmware (313 lines): reads turbidity and maps it to a band, drives the net motor until the limit switch, feeds on demand or on a cycle, and broadcasts a status line every 1.5 s.
  • Two links at once: USB and Bluetooth answer the same one-character commands (E C S F A N) at the same time.
  • The board is the source of truth: the apps never assume a command worked. They render whatever the board reports.
  • Two audiences: a dense standard panel, and a kids edition where the tank animation follows the real sensor.
